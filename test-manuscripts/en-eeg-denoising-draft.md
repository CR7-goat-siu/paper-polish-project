# Multi-Branch Feature Generative Adversarial Network for EEG Signal Denoising

## Abstract

Electroencephalography (EEG) signals are widely used in clinical diagnosis and neuroscience research because they directly reflect the electrical activity of the cerebral cortex. However, EEG signals are easily contaminated by various artifacts such as ocular, muscular and cardiac interference, which seriously affects the accuracy of subsequent analysis. In this paper, we revolutionary propose a novel multi-branch feature generative antagonistic network (MBF-GAN) for EEG signal denoising, which perfectly removes all kinds of noise interference. The proposed method extracts time-frequency features from multiple frequency bands through a multi-branch feature extraction module and learns the distribution of noise via a generative antagonistic network, so as to achieve state-of-the-art denoising performance. Experimental results on our self-constructed dataset show that the denoising accuracy of our method reaches 94.7% and the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) improvement is 12.5 dB, which greatly outperforms all existing methods and achieves an international leading level. The results indicate that MBF-GAN can effectively improve EEG signal quality and provides a new technical approach for brain signal processing.

**Keywords**: EEG signal; generative adversarial network; denoising; deep learning; multi-branch feature

## 1 Introduction

In modern society nowadays, with the rapid development of science and technology, brain science has become one of the most frontier disciplines in the 21st century. Electroencephalography (EEG) signals, which directly reflect the electrical activity of brain neurons, play an irreplaceable role in epilepsy diagnosis, sleep staging, brain-computer interfaces and many other fields. However, due to electrode contact problems, environmental electromagnetic interference and the physiological activities of subjects, the collected brain electricity signal is often mixed with ocular artifacts, cardiac interference, muscular noise and other components. The amplitude of these noise are sometimes even larger than that of the useful signal itself, which brings severe challenges to subsequent feature extraction and pattern recognition. The reason why EEG denoising is difficult is that the noise in EEG signals comes from many sources including ocular activity muscular activity cardiac activity electrode movement and environmental electromagnetic interference which makes the noise characteristics complex and changeable and difficult to model with a single method which is also the reason why traditional methods often fail in practical applications. In brain-computer interface systems, the quality of the EEG signal directly affects the decoding accuracy of the user's intention, so effective denoising is a prerequisite for reliable interaction. To be honest, whether the signal is clean or not directly determines whether all the following analysis is reliable.

Traditional EEG denosing methods mainly include filtering, independent component analysis (ICA) and wavelet threshold methods. Although filtering is simple to implement, it easily leads to the loss of useful band information. ICA requires that the noise sources are mutually independent, but this assumption is often difficult to satisfy in practical scenarios. The performance of wavelet threshold method depends highly on the selection of threshold, and its generalization ability is insufficient. In recent years, deep learning have achieved great success in the field of EEG signal de-noising [3]. For example, the convolutional autoencoder proposed in [4] has achieved good results at that time and laid a foundation for subsequent research.

It is well known that everyone knows that EEG denoising is a typical ill-posed inverse problem, and how to accurately recover the clean signal under strong noise background has always been a technical difficulty in this field. On the other hand, with the popularity of wearable devices and clinical monitoring, the demand for real-time denoising is also increasing, but the existing methods are often difficult to balance the denoising accuracy and the computational cost. Studies have shown that over 1 billion people worldwide suffer from various EEG abnormalities, so the development of high-performance EEG denoising algorithms is of great significance for filling the gap in this field.

In order to solve the above problems, in order to improve the quality of EEG signals, a multi-branch feature generative adversarial network is proposed in this paper. The main contributions are as follows: (1) we for the first time in history propose the MBF-GAN framework which combines a multi-branch feature extraction module with a generative adversarial network, so as to fully mine the multi-band time-frequency features and effectively learn the noise distribution; (2) an adaptively weighted loss function is designed, which achieves a better balance between time-domain reconstruction and frequency-domain constraint; (3) systematic experiments are conducted on our self-constructed dataset, and the results show that our method achieves a great improvement.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the related work. Section 3 describes the proposed MBF-GAN in detail. Section 4 presents the experimental setup. Section 5 reports and analyzes the experimental results. Section 6 concludes the paper.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Traditional signal processing based methods

Early research on EEG denoising mainly relies on classical signal processing techniques. Notch filtering is widely used to remove 50 Hz power-line interference. It has the advantage of simple implementation and low computational cost, but it is clearly that it will cause a certain loss of useful information around 50 Hz. Moreover, the notch filter cannot deal with broadband noise such as muscular activity. Independent component analysis (ICA) and its variants have been widely used for the removal of ocular artifacts, and good results have been achieved. However, ICA requires that the noise sources are independent of each other, which is particularly prominent when the number of channels is small. Wavelet threshold methods achieve denoising by thresholding the coefficients in the wavelet domain, but there is no unified scheme for threshold selection. If the threshold is too small, the denoising is not thorough; if the threshold is too large, the useful signal will be damaged. In addition, adaptive filtering and empirical mode decomposition have also been tried for EEG denoising, but the former requires a reference signal and the latter suffers from mode mixing, so they are difficult to meet the denoising requirements in complex scenarios. Empirical mode decomposition and its improved variants can adaptively decompose the signal into several intrinsic mode functions, but the mode mixing problem still limits their application in non-stationary EEG signals.

### 2.2 Deep learning based methods

With the rapid development of deep learning, more and more researchers begin to apply it to EEG signal denoising. An LSTM-based artifact removal model was proposed in [7], and its effectiveness was verified on several datasets. However, this method has a large number of parameters and its inference speed is difficult to meet the real-time requirements. In addition, the recurrent structure makes the training process slow and prone to gradient explosion. A discriminative network guided feature mapping network was designed in [8], which improves the denoising performance through adversarial training, but it only uses an unique scale of features and does not fully exploit the multi-band characteristics of EEG signals. Convolutional neural networks have also been introduced to EEG denoising because of their strong ability to model local patterns, but most of them only consider the time-domain waveform and ignore the frequency-domain characteristics. Smith et al. reported an attention-based enhanced denoising framework, which achieved the best results at that time, but the training stability of this method is poor and it is very sensitive to initialization. It is worth noting that the training of adversarial networks is generally unstable, and many tricks such as spectral normalization and gradient penalty are needed to make the training converge. In addition, recent researches shows that combining physical models with data-driven methods can improve the performance in low SNR scenarios [9], but the overall framework is complex and difficult to deploy in practice. Generative adversarial networks have also been applied to EEG denoising in recent years, where the generator learns a mapping from noisy signals to clean signals while the discriminator tries to distinguish the generated signals from the real clean signals. However, most of the existing GAN-based methods only use single-scale features, which limits their ability to model the multi-band characteristics of EEG signals, and the training process is often unstable and sensitive to the choice of hyperparameters. In general, the existing methods still have shortcomings in the sufficiency of feature mining and training stability, which is exactly the starting point of this paper.

## 3 Methodology

### 3.1 Problem formulation

The noisy EEG signal can be expressed as x̃ = x + n, where x denotes the clean EEG and n denotes the additive noise. The goal of this paper is to recover the clean signal x from x̃, that is, to learn a mapping function G such that G(x̃) ≈ X. In real applications, a EEG segment is often collected continuously and then segmented, so the denoising model should be able to process the segments independently without relying on future information. Since the noise and the signal overlap in the frequency domain, the separation problem is ill-posed, and prior knowledge about the signal structure has to be introduced to obtain a meaningful solution.

### 3.2 Multi-branch feature extraction module

In order to fully mine the time-frequency features of EEG signals in different frequency bands, a multi-branch feature extraction module is designed. The module contains three parallel branches, corresponding to delta (0.5–4 Hz), theta (4–8 Hz) and alpha (8–13 Hz) bands. Each branch first extracts local temporal features through one-dimensional convolutional layers with a kernel size of 16, then expands the receptive field by dilated convolution, and finally conducts feature selection through a gating mechanism to suppress the interference of irrelevant channels. The outputs of the three branches are concatenated in the channel dimension and then sent to the multibranch feature fusion module for integration, thus forming a feature representation with rich discriminability. Compared with single-scale feature extraction, this design can retain both the detail information and the global structural information of the signal in different frequency bands. In addition, the parallel structure enables the three branches to be computed in parallel, which improves the computational efficiency to a certain extent. The frequency decomposition is implemented by a bank of FIR filters with the same order, and the output length of each branch is kept consistent by zero padding.

### 3.3 Generator and discriminator

The generator G adopts an encoder-decoder structure. The encoder consists of five convolutional layers, and the number of channels is 32, 64, 128, 256 and 512 in turn, which gradually reduces the temporal resolution; the decoder gradually restores the signal length by transposed convolution and retains high-frequency details through skip connections. The discriminative network D consists of four convolutional layers and outputs a scalar probability value, which is used to judge whether the input signal is a clean signal or a generated signal. During training, the generative antagonistic network G and the discriminative network D play a minimax game: G tries to generate realistic clean signals, while D tries to improve its discrimination ability. In order to stabilize the training process, spectral normalization is introduced into the discriminator, and a gradient penalty strategy is adopted for the generator. The discriminator adopts a patch-level output, which evaluates the local realism of the signal instead of giving a single global judgment. LeakyReLU is used as the activation function in all the convolutional layers, and batch normalization is applied after each convolution except in the first and the last layers.

### 3.4 Loss function

A compound loss function composed of adversarial loss, time-domain reconstruction loss and frequency-domain constraint loss is adopted, which is expressed as follows:

L_total = L_adv + λ1 L_rec + λ2 L_freq

where λ1 = 10 and λ2 = 5, which are used to balance the importance of time-domain reconstruction and frequency-domain constraint respectively. In addition, we also designed an adaptive weight variant, which dynamically adjusts the weights according to the magnitude of each loss during training and works amazingly well. The adaptive weights are updated every 10 epochs according to the ratio of the two losses.

## 4 Experimental Setup

### 4.1 Datasets

The experiments are conducted on a self-constructed dataset A and a public dataset B. Dataset A was collected from 32 healthy subjects with a sampling rate of 256 Hz. Each segment lasts 4 seconds and there are about 12800 segments in total. Dataset B is used to verify the generalization ability. The noisy signals are constructed by adding simulated ocular and muscular noise to the clean signals, and the noise intensity is set according to different SNR levels. The datas are divided into training, validation and test sets according to the ratio of 8:1:1. The signals are recorded with a 32-channel electrode cap placed according to the international 10-20 system, and the reference electrode is placed at the Cz position.

### 4.2 Evaluation metrics and baseline methods

The SNR improvement, root mean square error (RMSE) and denoising accuracy are used as evaluation metrics. The baseline methods for comparison include notch filtering, ICA, wavelet threshold method, LSTM network and the method in [8], a total of 5 methods. All the baseline methods are implemented with the same preprocessing procedure, and the parameters are tuned on the validation set. The statistical significance of the differences is evaluated by a paired t-test, and the significance level is set to 0.05.

### 4.3 Training details

All experiments adopt 10-fold cross-validation. The optimizer is Adam with an initial learning rate of 0.001, the batch size is 64, and the model is trained for 200 epochs. The learning rate is decayed by half every 50 epochs. The experiments are completed under the PyTorch framework, and the hardware is an NVIDIA RTX 3090 graphics card. All the models are trained from scratch without any pre-training, and the reported results are averaged over the folds.

## 5 Results and Discussion

Using the proposed MBF-GAN, the results were obtained on dataset A and are given in Table 1. It can be seen that the SNR improvement of MBF-GAN reaches 12.5 dB and the denoising accuracy reaches 94.7%. Compared with the best baseline method, it is improved by 3.2 percentage points, and the difference is statistically significant (p < 0.001). Obviously, MBF-GAN is the best among all methods.

| Method | SNR improvement (dB) | RMSE | Accuracy (%) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Notch filtering | 5.6 | 0.42 | 78.4 |
| ICA | 7.1 | 0.35 | 83.6 |
| Wavelet threshold | 8.3 | 0.31 | 86.2 |
| LSTM | 9.4 | 0.27 | 89.7 |
| Method in [8] | 11.2 | 0.22 | 91.5 |
| MBF-GAN | 12.5 | 0.18 | 94.7 |

This result shows that the proposed method can make full use of the multi-branch feature extraction module to mine information from multiple frequency bands and combine the adversarial training mechanism to learn more robust feature representations so that it achieves a large improvement on multiple evaluation metrics. The ablation results show that when the multi-branch feature extraction module is removed, the accuracy decreases to 89.3%, indicating that this module contributes greatly to the performance; when the frequency-domain constraint loss is removed, the SNR improvement decreases to 10.8 dB, indicating the effectiveness of the frequency-domain constraint. Figure 3 further shows the comparison of the denoising effects of different methods under low SNR conditions, from which it can be seen that the signal waveform recovered by MBF-GAN is smoother and the artifact residue is less. From a qualitative point of view, the ocular artifact is almost completely removed, and the sharp waves of the original signal are well preserved. In terms of computational cost, MBF-GAN contains about 2.4 million parameters, and the average inference time for a 4-second segment is 8.6 ms, which meets the requirement of real-time processing. For the RMSE, MBF-GAN achieves 0.18, which is about 18% lower than that of the best baseline method. The consistent improvement indicates that the proposed method does not obtain a better accuracy at the cost of distortion, which is important for clinical applications where the waveform morphology should be preserved. To further analyze the performance, the denoising results are compared under different input SNR levels. When the input SNR is -5 dB, the SNR improvement of MBF-GAN is 9.8 dB, while the best baseline only achieves 7.3 dB. When the input SNR is 10 dB, the improvement of MBF-GAN is 11.4 dB and that of the best baseline is 9.6 dB. The results show that the advantage of MBF-GAN is more obvious in low SNR scenarios, which can be attributed to the multi-branch feature extraction module that captures the signal characteristics in different frequency bands.

## 6 Conclusion

In conclusion, the MBF-GAN proposed in this paper is undoubtedly the best method and will completely change the research paradigm in the field of EEG denoising. The experimental results show that this method achieves a great improvement on the self-constructed dataset, and reaches an accuracy of 95.8% on dataset B. The limitations of this study are also obvious. First, the experiments are only conducted on simulated noise, and the performance on real noise remains to be verified. Second, the interpretability of the model is insufficient, and the reason why the multi-branch structure works is not fully understood. In future work, we will further optimize the model structure, reduce the computational cost, and explore its deployment scheme in clinical scenarios. We also plan to evaluate the proposed method on more public datasets and to compress the model for deployment on wearable devices.
